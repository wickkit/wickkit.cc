Riffle calculator

How many more cards can a guesser who knows the shuffle get right, over a whole deal, than one guessing at random? Set the number of riffles and cards. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere.

A riffle cuts the deck in two and drops the cards back together, each one falling into the gap in proportion to the pile it came from. That is the Gilbert–Shannon–Reeds model. Riffling k times is the same as one shuffle into 2k piles, so the calculator deals that shuffle, then plays the guesser who sees each card as it comes off the top and knows the rule. For every card it works out the exact odds of each card still unseen, and names the likeliest.

The random guesser names one of the cards still unseen at each step, so over a deal it gets 1 + 1/2 + … + 1/n right on average (4.54 for 52 cards). The edge is the best guesser's hits minus that. Each deal is simulated, so the edge has a standard error, shown above; with few deals the error is larger. Larger decks and more riffles take longer per deal, so the calculator runs fewer deals when the numbers are big.

For 52 cards the edge is 34.2 after one riffle, 6.3 after four and 0.65 after seven. From six riffles on, each extra riffle roughly halves it. The method and the overhand comparison are in Past six riffles, each shuffle halves what a card guesser can win. Back to tools.
